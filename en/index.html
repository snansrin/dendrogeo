Global Tree Inventory & Carbon Data System

DendroGeo turns field measurements of individual trees (circumference at 1.30 m, derived DBH = circumference/π, height, species, GNSS position, photo evidence) into allometric carbon-stock estimates (Chave et al. 2014) and 10 m land-cover context for urban parks — with moderator approval, geofencing, Monte-Carlo uncertainty and fully disclosed QA/QC in reproducible scientific reports (DGR).

🌳 Individual-tree inventory

Every record carries point ID, species (canonical dictionary with sourced wood density ρ), raw circumference at 1.30 m, derived DBH, height, photo evidence and GNSS position. Raw girth_cm is preserved; dbh_cm = girth_cm / π. Allometry uses the unrounded derived DBH, while user-facing DBH is displayed to one decimal place.

⚖️ Carbon with disclosed uncertainty

AGB = 0.0673·(ρ·D²·H)^0.976; roots = 0.26·AGB; carbon = 0.47·biomass. Monte-Carlo (correlated model error, CV 22%) yields 95% confidence intervals per report.

🛰️ 10 m land-cover context

ESA WorldCover tiles are intersected with the park polygon (area-weighted border cells), with an area-balance QA gate (≤0.5%) before publication.

📄 DGR scientific reports

Each publication is an immutable, hash-verified document: metadata (DataCite 4.7-aligned), analysis fingerprint (engine, git commit, EPSG), QR to the permanent URL, citation & retraction notices. Author = the data contributor.

How is tree carbon stock calculated?

DendroGeo derives DBH from trunk circumference measured at 1.30 m above ground, then estimates biomass and carbon stock using tree height and wood density. The result is a stock estimate, not annual carbon sequestration or a personal carbon footprint. Model assumptions and measurement uncertainties are explained in the scientific methods.

Build a tree inventory with GPS position, species, circumference and height. Examine these records within the park boundary and five surface classes using urban park analysis, then export CSV, GeoJSON or QGIS files for your research workflow.

Urban park land-cover analysis

The accepted park surface uses five classes: green space, hard surface, buildings, water and bare ground. Pools and ornamental pools belong to water. ESA WorldCover raster results, Sentinel-2 spectral observations, OpenStreetMap geometries and user-accepted corrections are tracked separately. Relative NDVI describes the distribution within the same park; it is not a canopy-cover measurement. Read the scientific methods and limitations before interpreting results.
